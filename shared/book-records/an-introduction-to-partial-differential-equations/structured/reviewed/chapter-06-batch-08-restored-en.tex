\begin{Theorem}
\label{thm:bessel-parseval-orthonormal-expansion}
Let $\{x_i\}_{i\in I}$ be an orthonormal set in a Hilbert space $H$. Then\begin{enumerate}
\item $\sum_{i\in I}|(x_i,x)|^2\leq\|x\|^2$ for every $x\in X$.
\item Equality in 1 holds if and only if $x=\sum_{i\in I}(x_i,x)x_i$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{Theorem}

The inequality in 1 is referred to as Bessel\textquotesingle s inequality, or, in the case where equality holds, as Parseval\textquotesingle s equality.

\begin{Proof}
For finite subsets $J$ of $I$ we can use the fact that $\{x_i\}_{i\in I}$ is an orthonormal set to get the following:\[
\begin{aligned}
0&\leq\left\|x-\sum_{i\in J}(x_i,x)x_i\right\|^2\\
&=\left(x-\sum_{i\in J}(x_i,x)x_i,x-\sum_{l\in J}(x_l,x)x_l\right)\\
&=\|x\|^2-\sum_{i\in J}(x_i,x)(x,x_i)-\sum_{l\in J}(x,x_l)(x_l,x)\\
&\quad+\left(\sum_{i\in J}(x_i,x)x_i,\sum_{l\in J}(x_l,x)x_l\right)\\
&=\|x\|^2-2\sum_{i\in J}|(x_i,x)|^2+\sum_{i\in J}\sum_{l\in J}\overline{(x_i,x)}(x_l,x)(x_i,x_l)\\
&=\|x\|^2-\sum_{i\in J}|(x_i,x)|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]Hence $\sum_{i\in I}|(x_i,x)|^2$ exists, and Bessel\textquotesingle s inequality holds. By Lemma~\ref{lem:orthogonal-series-summability}, this implies that $\sum_{i\in I}(x_i,x)x_i$ also exists. Moreover, using the argument above,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orthonormal-expansion-remainder-norm-identity}
\left\|x-\sum_{i\in I}(x_i,x)x_i\right\|^2=\|x\|^2-\sum_{i\in I}|(x_i,x)|^2,
\end{equation}
and the second claim of the theorem is immediate.
\end{Proof}

\begin{Definition}
\label{def:hilbert-basis}
An orthonormal set $\{x_i\}_{i\in I}$ in a Hilbert space $H$ is called a \textbf{basis} if $x=\sum_{i\in I}(x_i,x)x_i$ for every $x\in H$.
\end{Definition}

In contrast to the usual definition of a vector space basis, we are allowing infinite series in the representation of $x$ as a linear combination of the $x_i$. If there is danger of confusion, then a basis in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:hilbert-basis} is called a Hilbert basis, whereas a vector space basis in the sense of finite linear combinations is called a Hamel basis. In the following, a basis is always a Hilbert basis.
