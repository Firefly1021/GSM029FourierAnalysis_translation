We can also show that for compact operators on a Hilbert space the converse of Theorem~\ref{thm:norm-limit-of-compact-operators} is true.

\begin{Theorem}
\label{thm:compact-hilbert-finite-rank-approximation}
Let $A\in\mathcal{L}(X,H)$ be compact. Then there is a sequence of operators $A_n\in\mathcal{L}(X,H)$, each having finite rank, such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-hilbert-finite-rank-approximation-limit}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|A_n-A\|=0.
\end{equation}

\end{Theorem}

\begingroup
\postdisplaypenalty=10000
\renewcommand{\endProof}{\par\nobreak\hfill$\blacksquare$\par\vspace{0.1cm}}
\begin{Proof}
We assume that $A$ does not have finite rank. Since $A$ is compact, its range is a countable union of precompact sets and hence separable. Let $\{\phi_i\}_{i=1}^\infty$ be an orthonormal basis for $\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}$. Let $P_n$ be the orthogonal projection from $\overline{\mathcal{R}(A)}$ onto
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-hilbert-finite-dimensional-projection-space}
M_n=\operatorname{span}\{\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_n\},
\end{equation}
and let $A_n=P_nA$. Obviously, $A_n$ has finite rank. We claim that $A_n\to A$. If not, there is (after taking an appropriate subsequence) a sequence $u_n\in X$ with $\|u_n\|=1$ and $\|(A-A_n)u_n\|\geq\varepsilon>0$. After taking a subsequence, we may assume that $Au_n$ converges to some limit $v$. We now find
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compact-hilbert-projection-approximation-contradiction}
\begin{aligned}
(A-A_n)u_n&=(I-P_n)Au_n\\
&=(I-P_n)v+(I-P_n)(Au_n-v).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Since the right-hand side of this equation converges to zero, we find that the left-hand side converges to zero, a contradiction.
\end{Proof}
\endgroup

\begin{Remark}
\label{rem:finite-rank-approximation-banach-space-caveat}
Theorem~\ref{thm:compact-hilbert-finite-rank-approximation} does not hold for general Banach spaces. On the other hand, we do not have to restrict the image space to be a Hilbert space. All we have actually used is the existence of finite-dimensional projections which converge strongly to the identity. Such projections actually exist in most of the Banach spaces which are important in applications.
\end{Remark}

The following result can be shown for general Banach spaces $X$ and $Y$.

\begin{Theorem}
\label{thm:compact-operator-adjoint-compact}
Let $A\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)$ be compact. Then $A^\times$ is compact.
\end{Theorem}

We ask the reader to prove this in the special case where $X$ and $Y$ are Hilbert spaces (Problem~\ref{ex:compact-adjoint-hilbert-proof}).
